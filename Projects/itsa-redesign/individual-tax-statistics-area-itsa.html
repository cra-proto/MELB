Individual Tax Statistics by Area (ITSA) reports

You may be looking for:

  • Check your refund status
  • Benefit and credit payment dates

The Individual Tax Statistics by Area (ITSA) reports show how many people file taxes, how much income they report, and which credits they claim, broken down by where they live in Canada.

Use ITSA to compare personal income tax data across cities, regions, postal areas, and provinces or territories. Each edition is published as PDF tables and as raw CSV data you can analyze in your own tools.

About the 2-year publication gap

The 2-year gap between the tax year and its published edition lets the CRA receive, process, and quality-check enough returns to produce a stable dataset. The typical timeline for the 2024 tax year:

  • April 30, 2025: filing deadline for most individuals
  • May to November 2025: the CRA processes late filings, T1 adjustments, and reassessments
  • November 30, 2025: data extraction cut-off (moved earlier for the 2026 Edition; older editions used June 30 of the publication year)
  • Early 2026: confidentiality suppression, data validation, table formatting, and publication

Publishing sooner would miss late filings and reassessments. Publishing later would delay data for researchers and analysts.

What ITSA can and cannot answer

These are the topics ITSA reports cover

  • Count tax filers in a given city, region, or postal area
  • See total and average income reported in a given geography
  • Break income down by source (employment, pension, investment, self-employment)
  • Compare tax filers across cities for a given tax year
  • Track how an area's tax data has changed across editions

These are not covered by ITSA

For the following, use another CRA report or system:

  • ITSA does not show Individual taxpayer records
  • ITSA does not show Business or corporate tax returns
  • ITSA does not show GST/HST registrant data
  • ITSA does not show Personal benefit payment data

See the Income statistics and GST/HST statistics hub for these other reports.

Geographic terms used in ITSA

ITSA reports tax data using geographic categories from the Statistics Canada 2021 Census. Definitions are below.

FSA
Forward Sortation Area. The first 3 characters of a postal code (for example, K1A for parts of Ottawa). Use this for postal-code-level analysis.
CMA
Census Metropolitan Area. A large city and its surrounding area. Must have at least 100,000 residents, with 50,000 in the urban core. Examples: Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver.
CA
Census Agglomeration. A smaller city and its surrounding area. Must have at least 10,000 residents in the urban core. Examples: Kingston, Moncton.
CMIZ
Census Metropolitan Influenced Zone. An area outside a CMA or CA, classified by the share of residents who commute into a CMA or CA for work.
Division / subdivision
Census division and census subdivision. Larger groupings used by Statistics Canada. A census division is roughly a county or regional district. A census subdivision is roughly a municipality.
Province / territory
The 13 Canadian provinces and territories. ITSA tables also include an "all Canada" total.
