Badges

A badge is a small numerical indicator attached to something — the count of new, unread, or outstanding items. It answers "how many?" at a glance: three new messages, two documents to review. It is a count, not a label and not an alert, so use it only where a number genuinely helps the reader.

Choose the right pattern

Badges, labels, and alerts all "call something out", but they answer different questions. Pick by what the reader needs to know.

Badge

Answers "how many?" — a count of new or outstanding items attached to an object, like a message count.

Label

Answers "what kind?" — a short word marking a status or category, like "New" or "Closed".

Alert

Answers "what's happening now?" — a temporary message about a change the reader must see.

Quick decision guide

  • Is it a count of items? → Badge
  • Is it a status or category word? → Label
  • Is it an urgent, temporary message? → Alert

What to include

  • A number that reflects a real, current count the reader cares about
  • A clear anchor — the object the count belongs to (an inbox, a task list)
  • Text that names what the number counts, not the digit alone

Guidance

  • Use a badge only for a count. If there's no number, you want a label, not a badge.
  • Keep counts meaningful and current — a stale "5 new" that never changes trains readers to ignore it.
  • Don't stack badges everywhere; a screen full of counts is as unhelpful as none.
  • Cap very large counts sensibly (for example, "99+") so the badge stays legible.

Accessibility

  • The number must be accompanied by text a screen reader can announce — "3 new messages", not just "3".
  • Don't rely on the badge's colour to convey meaning; the count and its label carry it.
  • Ensure the badge text meets contrast against its background.
  • If the count updates live, announce the change politely (for example, aria-live="polite") rather than stealing focus.

Examples

Good use of badges
  • "Mail 3" in My Account, with hidden text reading "3 new messages", tells the reader how many items are waiting.
  • A "Documents to submit 2" badge on a to-do list shows exactly how many items remain.
Badges to avoid
  • A red badge reading "New" with no number.
    Why it is wrong: "New" is a status, not a count. Use a label instead.
  • A bare badge showing "5" with nothing naming what it counts.
    Why it is wrong: a lone number is meaningless to a screen reader and ambiguous to everyone. Name what it counts.
  • A "12 new" badge that has said 12 for months.
    Why it is wrong: a stale count teaches readers to ignore badges. Keep it current or remove it.
