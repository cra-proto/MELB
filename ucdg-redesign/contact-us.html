Contact us

A contact page is where a reader lands when they can't finish online. Your job is to get them to the right channel fast — and, when they call, to make sure they have what they need before they're on the line. Lead with self-service, be honest about wait times, and never make someone read the whole page to find a phone number.

Choose the right pattern

There are two ways to build a contact page. Pick based on how many channels there are and how likely the reader is to pick the wrong one.

Basic contact us

A simple, laid-out set of channels.

Best for: one service with a handful of clear channels — online, phone, TTY — where the reader can easily pick the right one.

Contact us with interactive questions

Ask first, then show the right channel.

Best for: many phone lines or channels, where the reader would otherwise struggle to find the one that fits their situation.

Quick decision guide

  • A few clear channels the reader can pick from? → Basic contact us
  • Many lines, and picking the wrong one wastes the reader's time? → Interactive questions (see Interactive questions)
  • Does the reader mostly need one phone number? → make that number impossible to miss

What to include

  • Self-service first: what the reader can do in My Account (get information, submit documents) before any phone option
  • Phone and TTY numbers shown as their actual digits, with what each line is for
  • "Before you call": the identity details the reader needs ready — SIN, full name and date of birth, address, a recent tax document or being signed in to My Account
  • Hours of the line, and a note on representative calls (the person must consent or you must be authorized)
  • Expected wait times when they're long, so the reader can plan

Guidance

  • Lead with what the reader can do online. Many contact-page visitors can self-serve if you show them how first.
  • Make the phone number easy to find — don't bury it under paragraphs. If "find a phone number" is the real task, make it the headline.
  • Tell people what to have ready before they call. It shortens the call and spares them a second attempt.
  • Be honest about longer wait times rather than letting the reader discover them on hold.
  • Use plain channel labels — "Online", "By phone", "By mail" — and keep them in a consistent order across contact pages.
  • Present the number so it works on a phone (tap-to-call) and reads clearly for someone dialing manually.

Accessibility

  • Always provide a TTY number alongside the voice line, and note the operator-assisted relay option.
  • Write phone numbers as real text, not an image, so they can be read aloud and tapped to call.
  • If a link opens the phone or TTY application, say so in the link text — an unexpected app switch is disorienting.
  • Structure channels with real headings so a screen-reader user can jump straight to "By phone".
  • Don't rely on layout columns alone to separate channels; each needs its own heading.

Examples

Compare contact pages side by side

Good contact pages
  • A DTC contact page leads with "Use My Account to get tax credit information and submit documents", then "By phone" with the number, a "Before you call" list (SIN, name, date of birth, address), and the line's hours.
  • A page with many lines uses an interactive question — "What do you need help with?" — and reveals only the phone number for the reader's situation.
  • A notice at the top: "Expect longer wait times during tax season (March to May)", so the reader can plan.
Contact pages to avoid
  • Four paragraphs of policy before any phone number appears.
    Why it is wrong: the reader came for the number. Lead with self-service, then make the number easy to find.
  • The phone number shown as an image.
    Why it is wrong: it can't be read by a screen reader or tapped to call. Use real text.
  • A voice number with no TTY line and no relay note.
    Why it is wrong: it excludes people who can't use a voice line. Always provide TTY and relay options.
  • Twelve phone lines listed flat, leaving the reader to guess which applies.
    Why it is wrong: too many undifferentiated options. Use an interactive question to route them.
