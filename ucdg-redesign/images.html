Images

An image should do a job the text can't — show what a form looks like, illustrate a step, make a page easier to understand. On a task-focused CRA page, most decorative images slow the reader down. Use images that inform, give every meaningful image a text alternative, and never put information only in a picture.

Choose the right pattern

Decide what the image is for before you add it. If it doesn't help the reader do their task, it probably doesn't belong.

  • Informative image

    Shows something the reader needs to see — a screenshot of where to click, a diagram of a process. Needs alt text.

  • Decorative image

    Adds visual interest but no information. Use sparingly, and hide it from assistive technology (empty alt).

  • Text instead

    Often the best answer. If the "image" is really words — a table screenshot, text on a graphic — use real text.

Quick decision guide

  • Does the reader need to see it to do the task? → Informative image with alt text
  • Is it just visual interest? → Decorative, hidden from assistive tech (or cut it)
  • Is the content actually text inside a picture? → use real text

What to include

  • A clear purpose — the image shows something the words can't
  • Alternative text that conveys the image's meaning for anyone who can't see it
  • An appropriately sized, responsive image that reflows on small screens

Guidance

  • Don't put important information only in an image. Anything the reader must know goes in the text too.
  • Avoid text baked into images — it can't be resized, translated, searched, or read aloud.
  • Keep decorative images to a minimum on task pages; they add load and distraction.
  • Use images at a size that stays legible and doesn't force the page to scroll sideways on a phone.

Accessibility

  • Every informative image needs alt text that conveys its meaning, not a literal description of pixels.
  • Decorative images take an empty alt="" so screen readers skip them.
  • Provide a text equivalent for any information shown in a diagram or infographic.
  • Don't rely on colour within an image to carry meaning without another cue.

Examples

Compare images side by side

Good use of images
  • A screenshot showing exactly where to find "Submit documents" in My Account, with alt text describing the location.
  • A diagram of the application process, paired with the same steps written out as a numbered list.
Images to avoid
  • The eligibility rules presented only as text inside a graphic.
    Why it is wrong: it can't be resized, translated, searched, or read aloud. Use real text.
  • A large decorative stock photo at the top of a filing page.
    Why it is wrong: it adds load and pushes the task down the page without helping. Cut it.
  • An informative screenshot with no alt text.
    Why it is wrong: readers who can't see it get nothing. Add alt text conveying its meaning.
