Page structure

Components that give a page its spine. Use these when you are deciding how a whole page is laid out, how a reader moves through it, how its headings are named and nested, and whether a long task should live on one page or be split across a guided series of pages.

Components in this family

Layout
The fixed frame every CRA page sits in — the Government of Canada banner, the footer, and the grid that sets page width at each screen size
Table of contents
An "On this page" list that lets a reader jump to a section of a long page, or a directory that links out to the subpages of a product
Headings and titles
How to write and nest the page title (H1) and the headings below it so readers can scan, and screen readers can navigate
Subway pattern
A multi-page pattern that breaks a complex product into a series of related steps with persistent navigation — used for eligibility and application journeys

Choosing between them

Start with Layout

The banner, footer, and grid are fixed for every page. Read this first so you know what you do not control before you design the parts you do.

Then Headings and titles

Name the page and its sections. Good headings are what let a reader — and a screen reader — scan the page and find their task.

Add a Table of contents when the page is long

If a reader has to scroll more than two or three times to see everything, give them an "On this page" list to jump with.

Reach for the Subway pattern only for a task journey

When one page cannot hold a whole product — understand, check eligibility, apply, follow up — split it into subway steps. Never use it for unrelated pages.
