Pagination

Pagination breaks a long set of items — search results, a directory, a report — into numbered pages the reader moves through. Use it when a single page would be too long to load or scan, and give the reader clear controls to move and to know where they are. Do not confuse it with splitting a single document across pages, which usually reads better as one page with an "On this page" list.

Choose the right pattern

Decide by what you're breaking up: a long list of results, or a single piece of content.

Pagination

Use for a long list of items — results, a directory — where loading or scanning all of it at once is impractical.

Single page + TOC

Use for one long document — keep it on one page with an "On this page" list to jump within it.

Filter or search

Often better than paging — let the reader narrow a long list to what they want rather than flipping pages.

Quick decision guide

  • A long list of results or entries? → Pagination (ideally with a filter too)
  • One long document? → Single page with a TOC
  • A task done in steps? → the subway pattern, not pagination

What to include

  • Previous and next controls, plus numbered page links for longer sets
  • A clear indication of the current page and the total (for example, "Page 2 of 8")
  • Large, tappable targets for each control
  • Sensible page sizes, so the reader isn't flipping every few items

Guidance

  • Show where the reader is and how much is left. "Page 2 of 8" reassures; a bare "Next" doesn't.
  • Pair pagination with a filter or search for long lists — flipping through dozens of pages is nobody's idea of finding something.
  • Don't paginate a single short document just to add page views.
  • Keep controls in the same place on every page so they're predictable.

Accessibility

  • Mark up pagination as a navigation region with an accessible name, and the current page with aria-current="page".
  • Controls must be real links or buttons, reachable and operable by keyboard.
  • Give "Previous" and "Next" accessible text that says what they page through, not just an arrow.
  • Don't rely on colour alone to show the current page.

Examples

Compare pagination side by side

Good use of pagination
  • A forms-and-publications directory shows 20 per page with numbered pages, a filter box, and "Page 3 of 12" clearly shown.
  • Search results with Previous/Next and page numbers, the current page marked for screen readers.
Pagination to avoid
  • A single guide chopped into eight numbered pages with Next buttons.
    Why it is wrong: one document reads better as one page. Use a table of contents.
  • Pagination with only a "Next" arrow and no sense of how many pages remain.
    Why it is wrong: the reader can't tell where they are or when they'll finish. Show "Page X of Y".
  • A 60-page result set with no filter or search.
    Why it is wrong: flipping 60 pages is not finding. Add a filter.
