Text editing effects

A text editing effect hides secondary text behind a control the reader can expand — an accordion or a "show more" toggle. It is useful when a page has genuinely optional detail that most readers can skip. It is harmful when it hides something the reader actually needs. Reach for it rarely.

When to use it

  • Use it only for content that is genuinely secondary — extra detail, an edge case, a long example — that most readers do not need to complete their task.
  • Give every collapsed section a clear label that says exactly what is inside, so a reader can decide whether to open it.
  • Keep the default state closed only when closed is the right starting point for most readers.

Guidance

  • Never hide content the reader needs to finish their task — fees, deadlines, eligibility, or required steps must always be visible.
  • Do not nest an effect inside another effect. One level of "expand" is the most a reader will tolerate.
  • Do not hide an alert inside a collapsed section — an alert must be visible on page load.
  • Write the toggle label as a description of the content, not "Click here" or "More".
  • If most readers will open it anyway, don't collapse it — just show the content.

Accessibility

  • The toggle must be a real button, reachable and operable by keyboard, with its expanded/collapsed state exposed to assistive technology (for example, aria-expanded).
  • Collapsed content must still be found by in-page search and by screen readers when expanded — do not remove it from the accessibility tree entirely.
  • Do not rely on a chevron or colour alone to signal state; the state must be programmatically available.
  • Make sure the content reads sensibly if JavaScript is off — ideally the sections are simply expanded.

Examples

Compare text editing effects side by side

Good use of a reveal effect
  • A "How to apply" page keeps the steps visible and puts a rarely needed edge case — "If you applied before 2023" — behind a labelled expandable section.
  • A long list of accepted document types is collapsed under "See the full list of accepted documents", so the common path stays short.
Reveal effects to avoid
  • The filing deadline is hidden inside a collapsed "Details" accordion.
    Why it is wrong: the deadline is essential. Never hide content the reader needs to act.
  • A toggle labelled "Click here for more".
    Why it is wrong: the label doesn't say what's inside, so the reader can't decide whether to open it.
  • An accordion nested three levels deep to fit a complex scenario.
    Why it is wrong: nested reveals bury content. If it needs that much structure, it belongs on its own page.
