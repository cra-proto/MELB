Text editing effects

Text editing effects show that text has been changed or is being called out as an edit — highlighted, deleted, struck through, inserted, or redacted. They carry a specific editorial meaning, so use them for that meaning, not for plain emphasis. Most of them make a page harder to read, so use them sparingly and temporarily.

The five effects

  • Marked (<mark>) — highlights text for reference, such as matching search terms or text shown in relation to something else.
  • Deleted (<del>) — shows text that was recently removed, such as a change to an official document.
  • Strikethrough (<s>) — indicates text that is no longer accurate or relevant, such as an old price shown next to a new one.
  • Inserted (<ins>) — shows text that was recently added. Use with caution (see Guidance).
  • Redacted — indicates content that is protected or unavailable to view; inline for a few words, full-width for a whole line, paragraph, list, or table.

Guidance

  • Use marked text only to call out text for reference — never as a substitute for bold. It is not a highlighter for key words or amounts.
  • Use deleted and strikethrough sparingly; both make a page harder to read. Reserve them for showing changes to an official document (meeting minutes, a law) or as a temporary effect.
  • Use strikethrough for corrections where both versions must be seen — for example, an old and a new price.
  • Do not use inserted text — its underline mimics a link and readers will try to click it. Non-links must not look like links.
  • Use redacted for protected content only. Do not use strikethrough to fake a redaction — redaction has its own treatment.

Accessibility

  • The visual effect is not enough on its own. For strikethrough replacements, include hidden text (class="wb-inv") describing the original and replacement so screen-reader users understand the change.
  • Never rely on colour or a line alone to convey that text was deleted, inserted, or redacted — the meaning must be available to assistive technology.
  • Do not use inserted text — its link-like underline is a usability failure for everyone, and especially confusing with a screen reader.
  • For a full-width redaction, make sure the redacted region is announced as protected or unavailable, not read aloud as blank.

Good use of editing effects
  • Search results highlight the matching term with marked text so the reader sees why each result matched.
  • A price correction shows both values: $50 $45, with hidden text naming the old and new amounts for screen readers.
  • A protected field on an account summary is shown as an inline [redacted] region.
Editing effects to avoid
  • Using marked text to highlight an important amount for emphasis.
    Why it is wrong: marked text means "called out for reference", not emphasis. Use bold for a key word.
  • Using inserted text (underlined) to show a new clause.
    Why it is wrong: it looks like a link and readers will try to click it. Don't use inserted text.
  • Striking through a name to "redact" it.
    Why it is wrong: strikethrough still shows the text. Use the redacted treatment for protected content.
