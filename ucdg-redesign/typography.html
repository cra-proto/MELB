Typography

Typography — the fonts, sizes, and spacing of your text — is set for you by the GCWeb design system. You do not choose fonts or heading sizes; they arrive with the template. What you need to know is what you inherit, so you use the right heading levels, and the one thing you do control: keeping lines short enough to read.

What the design system sets

Heading font — Lato

Headings render in Lato. The size is tied to the heading level, so choosing the right level (see Headings and titles) is what gives you the right size — never restyle a heading to fake a different level.

Body font — Noto Sans

Body text renders in Noto Sans at 20 px, the same size on every screen. Do not shrink body text to fit more on the page.

Link appearance

Links in the content area are underlined. The only exceptions are links styled as buttons, and links in the banner and footer. See Links for the full guidance.

Line length (what you control)

Keep a line of text to a maximum of about 65 characters. Beyond that, the eye loses its place jumping back to the start of the next line, and comprehension drops.

  • The limit is about the text, not the page. A page layout can be wider than 65 characters; the line of running text should not be.
  • On a wide desktop, constrain the reading measure of body paragraphs rather than letting them stretch the full container width.

Guidance

  • Do not change fonts, heading sizes, or body size. They are set by the design system and must stay consistent across Canada.ca.
  • Get size by using the correct heading level, not by restyling. An H3 that "looks too big" is a signal your structure is wrong, not the size.
  • Do not use ALL CAPS for emphasis or headings — it is harder to scan and screen readers may read it letter by letter.
  • Let text reflow. Never set fixed widths or manual line breaks that stop text from wrapping on small screens.

Accessibility

  • The 20 px body size and 65-character measure exist for readability — do not override them to cram in content.
  • Text must stay legible and reflow at 200% and 400% zoom; fixed pixel widths break this.
  • Never convey meaning through font styling alone. Emphasis needs a word or structure behind it, not just a look (see Text formatting).
  • Use real headings for structure so screen-reader users can navigate by heading level.

Examples

Working with the type system

Good use of typography
  • A benefits guide uses H2 for each section and H3 for sub-points, letting the design system's Lato sizes carry the hierarchy — no manual restyling.
  • On a wide desktop, the body paragraphs of a GST/HST page are held to a comfortable measure instead of running the full 1170 px container.
  • Body text stays at 20 px Noto Sans throughout, so the page reads consistently on a phone and a desktop.
Typography mistakes to avoid
  • A team styles an H4 to look like an H2 "because it needed more weight."
    Why it is wrong: it breaks the heading hierarchy for screen readers. Use the correct level and let the system size it.
  • Body text is dropped to 14 px to fit a long table on one screen.
    Why it is wrong: it fails readability and zoom requirements. Simplify the content instead of shrinking the type.
  • A section heading is written in ALL CAPS for emphasis.
    Why it is wrong: caps are harder to scan and some screen readers spell them out. Use sentence case and a real heading.
  • A full-width paragraph on desktop runs 140 characters per line.
    Why it is wrong: past ~65 characters the reader loses their place. Constrain the reading measure.
