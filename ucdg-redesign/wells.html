Wells

A well is a light grey box that sets a piece of content apart from the flow of the page — a summary, a related-links group, a small call to action. It's a gentle spotlight. It works because it's used rarely: a page full of grey boxes has no emphasis left, and a well is never a substitute for an alert when something is urgent.

Choose the right pattern

A well groups and lightly highlights. If you need urgency or a strong signal, a well is the wrong tool.

  • Well

    Use to group and gently highlight a small block — a summary, related links, a secondary call to action.

  • Alert

    Use when the content is urgent or a warning — a well is too quiet for that.

  • Plain content

    Most content needs no box. If it's part of the normal reading flow, leave it in the flow.

Quick decision guide

  • Grouping a small block and giving it a gentle lift? → Well
  • Is it urgent or a warning? → Alert
  • Is it just normal content? → No box

What to include

  • A small, self-contained block of related content
  • A heading inside the well when it groups a set of things, so its purpose is clear
  • Enough contrast that the content inside stays readable on the grey background

Guidance

  • Use wells sparingly. One or two on a page draw the eye; a dozen cancel each other out.
  • Don't use a well to signal urgency — that's an alert's job.
  • Don't nest wells or stack them wall to wall; the effect depends on contrast with plain content around it.
  • Keep the content inside short — a well is a spotlight, not a container for half the page.

Accessibility

  • The grey background is decorative — the meaning must come from the content and its heading, not the box.
  • Ensure text inside the well meets contrast against the grey.
  • Give a grouping well a heading so its purpose is announced, rather than relying on the visual box alone.

Good use of wells
  • A "What you'll need before you start" summary set in a well at the top of a task page, so it reads as a quick checklist apart from the steps.
  • A single "Related information" links group in a well at the end of a page.
Wells to avoid
  • A service disruption message placed in a plain well.
    Why it is wrong: a disruption is urgent. Use an alert, which signals urgency; a well is too quiet.
  • A page where every section sits in its own grey well.
    Why it is wrong: when everything is boxed, the box means nothing. Reserve wells for what should stand apart.
